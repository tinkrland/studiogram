can you take product photos before the product exists? if it is cast
metal, yes: the file your caster needs is already a photograph waiting to
happen. the short answer:

lost-wax casting starts from a 3d-printed master, which starts from a cad
mesh. that mesh is the exact geometry of the piece you will ship. a
deterministic render of that mesh is a product photo in every sense that
matters: same prongs, same stone seats, same proportions, because it is
the same geometry, not a drawing of it. made-to-order jewelers have been
rendering from cad for years; the change is doing it as photography, on
a subject, in a scene.

## what the studio adds over a raw cad render

a cad viewport screenshot looks like a cad viewport. a photo needs a
subject, light, and a composition. the studio puts the mesh on a rigged
hand with a real 33-bone skeleton: pose the fingers, stack the bands,
choose from 19 nail shapes, set the skin tone, put a necklace on a bust
with drape that sags the way chains sag. light it, choose the crop, pin
the camera. captures are reproducible: re-open the scene and the same
state reconstructs the same pixels, byte for byte.

## what this replaces

a single on-model shoot with a hand model commonly runs $200 to $5,000
and has to be scheduled around a finished piece existing. for custom and
preorder work the piece may exist exactly once. the alternative until now
was either shooting your one precious piece in a rented hour, or
generating imagery with a diffusion model and hoping the prongs come out
right. the render path does not hope: the mesh is the proof.

## the honest limits

- you need the mesh. photo-only sellers have no onramp yet; that is a
  real gap, stated plainly.
- a render is synthetic media. disclose it where the platform asks. the
  disclosure is easy to make honestly when the image is the product.
- the studio photographs small, wearable, metal-first goods today:
  rings, earrings, necklaces, bracelets. it is not a general product
  photographer yet.
