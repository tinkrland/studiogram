# icp: buyer segments, in order of readiness

segments as a list, never a composite. hypothesis until a live surface
measures anything.

## 1. the made-to-order jeweler (beachhead)

already has the mesh: lost-wax casting starts from a 3d-printed master, so
the cad file exists before the product does. pain: photographing one-off
pieces before or instead of photographing them at all; listing photos for
custom and preorder work; showing the customer what they are buying before
it is cast. studiogram renders their exact geometry, so the photo can
precede the object. exactness is the whole value; no hallucination
tolerance in fine jewelry. found where: cad and casting communities, etsy
and shopify jewelry sellers doing custom work, bench jewelers on instagram.

## 2. the burned seller (the flag segment)

dropshippers and small ecommerce sellers who have been stung by the 2026
label wave: tiktok ad rejections or account flags, etsy image compliance
sweeps (august 2026), meta "ai info" labels shrinking reach. they want
tweakable, repeatable product imagery without generation risk. honest
caveat: most do not have meshes. this segment converts only after a
photo-to-mesh onramp or template library exists. until then they are the
audience for the answer pages, not the product.

## 3. the small jewelry brand running preorder or made-to-order drops

needs listing and social imagery on a schedule, wants the same piece in
many crops (4:5, 9:16, 1:1 are all studio presets now), cannot book a
hand model every drop. natural social fit: the capture-set to pinterest
and instagram flows already exist on the commerce branch.

## explicitly not the icp (yet)

- buyers with photos only and no mesh: no onramp today.
- high-volume dropshipping at scale: needs the onramp plus an api-first
  batch mode, neither built.
- anyone wanting generative surprise: wrong product, by design.
